| KQ23 | A code-derived behavioural claim about a window (for example "a first SIGTERM ends in `return 0`") is admitted in two parts. **Arm A (claim and deciding set).** (i) The claim text (`scope` or `teaches`) names the window by two endpoints, each cited by line, and states the outcome inside it. (iii) The read that selects the branch toward the outcome is cited. (iv) The window endpoints are cited. **Arm B (path completeness).** Every element of closure C (defined below) is cited by a pinned line range, or listed as unread (name and call site) or as a data-work boundary (name and pinned range). Closure C is generated by this rule: from the trigger body and the outcome path, expand every call in an executed body; expand a callee with a pinned definition only if its body references the decided state, a worker or thread, a join or stop, a terminate, start or shutdown, or `exit`; any other pinned callee is a data-work boundary; any callee with no pinned definition is unread. C++ and POSIX primitives (lock and unlock, condition wait and notify, thread join, `std::function` invocation, `sigaction`, `GGML_ASSERT`) and logging macros are a named class not admitted as evidence. | Arm A is not met when the claim names no end-endpoint or states an outcome that is not inside a named window, or when any element of (ii)–(iv) is missing: the claim is then not admitted. Arm B is not met when an element of C is omitted without being listed: the path claim is not admitted. A row that meets A and fails B keeps its A claim and loses only the path claim. A data-work boundary's duration is never admitted. | `lessons/round-17.md` M8 (line 309): S043 `teaches` overstated the SIGTERM path; S047 supersedes it (lines 222-225). |
