## 4. Contested scope for adjacency (report only, never absorb)

1. **C2 `pin-watch` "reports newer upstream releases for pinned engines" — currently unique.** No sibling tool
   queries upstream (no `ls-remote`, no `releases`/`tags` API, no `newer` logic in any sibling `tools/`).
2. **Mechanism adjacency — git-ref pinning.** `souji-rev-pin.py` resolves durable refs (heads/tags/remotes,
   peeled) exactly like a release-watch would; but it pins the lane's own evidence-runner revisions, and does not
   contact upstream. Also `mori-pin-check.py` (gate-registry pins) and `tameshi pin-disposition.py` (evidence
   manifests) share the "pin" vocabulary and git-ref resolution while remaining internal.
3. **Observation adjacency — upstream mutation watching.** `mori-source-drift.py` re-fetches SOURCES.md rows and
   flags drift of already-cited copies. It shares kama's *upstream observation* surface but differs in trigger
   (byte-drift of current content vs. detection of a *newer release* of a pinned engine) and in scope (all cited
   rows vs. the lane's pinned engines). If kama C2 later claims "the lane that watches upstream sources", that
   claim must be scoped to *release-level* watching to avoid overlapping mori's drift observation.
4. **Data-level convention — `git ls-remote` SOURCES rows are not kama-exclusive.** At least five sibling lanes
   (kaidoku, kotoba, kumitate, mado, souji) plus kama embed `git ls-remote` as row provenance. Any claim that
   kama "owns" that practice would be false; the convention is fleet-wide and per-row one-off.
