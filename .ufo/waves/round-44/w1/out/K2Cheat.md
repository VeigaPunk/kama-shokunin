# K2Cheat — C2 shortcut attack (round 44, lane kama, seat K2Cheat)

**Verdict: the frozen C2 counter prints `met=True` for a ledger nobody read.** The unjudged automatic
draft (already `valid=204` on its own, exactly the prereg control `junk-c2-noscorecard`) plus a
SCORECARD section generated by a 24-line script from that same ledger reaches
`met=True agree=61/72 table_missing=0 summary=1 axes=1`; the published agreement figure is steerable
between **58 and 77 of 87 keys purely by which cited line is chosen**, and one cited line can support
three different verdicts, all valid. `met=True` proves file/rev/path identity, line membership and
internal arithmetic of the scorecard — it proves nothing about whether either side's verdict was
read, nor that the lane's self-assessment is calibrated. This is a defect in the frozen evidence
rule, not in the lane's tree: no lane file was edited.

## 2. What `met=True` does and does not prove

**Proves** (per the frozen code, `tools/round44-verdict-ledger-audit.py:91-245`):
1. 204 rows exist, one per (round 10–43, trend, side), with the side's exact repo/rev/path.
2. Each cited `line` is in range and the counter's tokenizer finds the claimed verdict word for the
   claimed trend on it (nearest preceding trend word, anywhere on the line, lines 91–105).
3. The `### Round-44 calibration ledger` table's `(round,trend,self,outside)` set equals the mismatch
   set *implied by the ledger's own verdict cells* (lines 226–229); a line under the heading contains
   the exact `agreement=…` substring (237); an `## Axes` row has `calibration` in cell 1 and the
   ledger path somewhere on the line (245).

**Does not prove:**
- that the cited line is the file's verdict sentence rather than a quotation, a negation, an example,
  or a word about another subject;
- that the verdict read from that line is the one a reader would take (the same line accepts 1–3
  distinct verdicts: 19 key-sides accept ≥3);
- that the SCORECARD table was written from reading rather than emitted from the ledger (it is a pure
  function of the ledger);
- that the notes mean anything (non-absent notes are unconstrained — `met=True` with empty notes);
- that the agreement figure is stable: it moves with citation choice between 58/87 and 77/87, and the
  denominator moves too (the draft's `n=72` excludes 15 `other` keys; the honest reading is `n=87`);
- anything about calibration: no check ties a verdict to the round's actual work.

## 3. Shortcut catalogue

### S1 — draft ledger + scorecard generated from it (zero lines read) → `met=True`
`gen_scorecard.py` (24 lines) reads `registry/verdict-ledger.tsv`, computes the mismatch set with the
counter's own ORDER, emits 26 table rows + the summary line, and adds
`| calibration | 7 | \`registry/verdict-ledger.tsv\` (round-44 C2) | round 44 |` under `## Axes`.
Receipt `draft-plus-derived-scorecard.out`, `draft-derived-scorecard.diff` (51 lines), exit 0,
`met=True`. Refusing rule: none exists — A4 (table must carry verbatim bictext) proposed.

### S2 — mechanical first-hit / last-hit ledgers (no interpretation at all) → `met=True`
`firsthit.py` emits, per key, the first (or last) line that names the trend and that line's first
verdict word (`mixed` when the line carries two); then the S1 table. Receipts `first-hit.out` (`agree=59`, over=5) and `last-hit.out`
(`agree=61`, over=3), both exit 0, `met=True`. The draft is the last-hit variant, so the lane's
"automatic last hit" draft and a two-line tool that never interprets prose are indistinguishable to
the counter. Refusing rule: A1/A2/A5.

### S3 — verdict steering: the same cited line supports 1–3 verdicts, all valid
`ambiguity.txt`: 22 of 189 key-sides accept more than one verdict from the file, 19 of them ≥3, mostly
from a *single* line. Receipts, all `met=True`, only the stated row changed:

Choosing, for every key, an admissible reading that makes the two sides agree gives

### S4 — negation read as verdict (already present in the frozen draft)
`9d9cc93e:corpus/lessons/round-25-verifier-spotcheck.md:193` = *"entry's fact false or stale, so
quality is not harmed."* The draft records `25 quality outside = harmed` and the frozen counter
returns `VALID`; the same file's own verdict line is `:187 - **Quality: unchanged.**`. The 6-line
tokenizer sees `quality` then `harmed` and cannot see `not`. Refusing rule: A1 (measured: that row
becomes invalid under A1, `amend-A1-headline.out`).

### S5 — quotation read as verdict
`9d9cc93e:corpus/lessons/round-29-verifier-spotcheck.md:69` is
`- **expansion: improved** — … Note: the lane self-reported \`unchanged\` …`; the draft records
`mixed` for it (`v29e-unchanged-quoted.out`, `met=True`), because the quoted *other side's* word is
tokenized as a verdict. `9d9cc93e:corpus/lessons/round-43-verifier-spotcheck.md:96` contains
*"Not \`improved\` by the trend's definition"*, so `improved` is also a valid reading there
(`v43q-improved.out`). Refusing rule: A1.

### S6 — the three scorecard legs are not evidence about the files
- (a) summary substring (line 237): a **disclaimer** line — *"these figures prove only token
  consistency"* — plus the required figures still gives `summary=1 met=True`
  (`v43q-disclaim.out`).
- (b) note column: empty notes on every non-absent row still give `valid=204 met=True`
  (`empty-notes-bare-axes.out`); only absent notes are length-checked (lines 172–176).
- (c) axes leg (line 245): `| calibration | - | \`registry/verdict-ledger.tsv\` | round 44 |` (no
  score, no evidence) gives `axes=1 met=True`; a table row with a fabricated 5th column
  (`fabricated-extra-cell |`) also passes (`v43q-extracell.out`) — only 4 cells are parsed
  (lines 226–229). Refusing rules: A3, A4, A6.

### S7 — the `absent` escape (bounded, worth stating)
15 of 102 keys never enter the comparison: 12 because the outside file does not exist at the base
(rounds 20–23) and 3 because the round-38 self report files its trends as
`calibration / coverage / operability` (`b0fd7b7f:.ufo/reports/round-38/kama.md:38-42`), not as
`expansion / quality / discernment`. This is defensible as written, but it means the claim's "30
outside closures" comparison runs over 87 keys and a future report can drop out of the comparison by
never writing the three trend words. Refusing rule: A7.

### What the counter *does* refuse (tested, so the attack is not vacuous)
Blanket `unchanged` everywhere → `valid=92 bad=112 met=False` (`blanket-unchanged.out`, exit 1);
`junk-c2-allimproved` in the prereg is the same class. So the counter does catch verdict/label
mismatch; it does not catch *which* admissible label was chosen, nor its provenance.

## 5. Consequence for the C2 predicate as frozen

The prereg predicate is *"every round-10..43 trend verdict, self and outside, is ledgered on a cited
line that carries it, and SCORECARD names every disagreement with the agreement figures; missing=0."*
`met=True` is satisfied by a cited line that *carries the word*, not by a line that *is* the verdict —
so the predicate as frozen is satisfiable without the reading it asserts. Two figures are stable and
should be what the lane publishes if it keeps the rule: with the headline reading (the cited line's own
verdict word) and the one negation row re-cited from `…round-25-verifier-spotcheck.md:193` to `:187`,
the ledger is `agree=68/87 overclaim=2 underclaim=17 other=0 absent=15` under the **frozen** counter
(receipt `corrected-frozen.out`, exit 0 `met=True`) and under A1 (`amend-A1-corrected.out`), not
`61/72`; the draft's 15 `other` rows hide 6
agreements and 9 underclaims, and its `n=72` denominator inflates the rate from 78.2% to 84.7%.

The frozen controls stop one step short of the attack: `junk-c2-noscorecard` already records the
draft at `valid=204`, but no control adds the ledger-derived scorecard, which is the only missing
piece for `met=True`. A control `junk-c2-draftplusderived` (expected `met=True`) would have made the
gap visible at freeze; it is a finding for the L1, not something to change in place.
