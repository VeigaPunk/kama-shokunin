## 2. Patterns in Maintaining Banner / Planned / Saturation

The audit reveals a fundamental architectural divide between two patterns across the fleet:

| Dimension | Pattern A: Append-and-Abandon (`tameshi`, `mori`, `chousa`, `kake`, `kotodama`) | Pattern B: Dynamic Reconciled Head (`kama`, partially `kumitate`) |
|---|---|---|
| **Head `## Axes` Table** | Treated as historical baseline (frozen at round 1, 2, or 24), or completely omitted (`kotodama`). | Continuously derived and re-verified against live commit bytes at every round. |
| **`## Planned`** | Written at round 1 or 2 and never updated in the head. New plans are appended inside round logs at the end of the file. | Must name `### Planned (round <maxN+1>)`, which must exist as an active line followed by a numbered item. |
| **`## Saturation`** | Statically declared in early rounds (r1, r12, r20) and abandoned. | Dynamically derived from the score vector, canon capability first-working rounds, and stagnation nudge rule. |
| **`## Retired`** | Static "None" or missing. | Dynamically asserts exact active axes count and capability count. |

### What `kama`'s C1 Offers Sibling Lanes:
- **Zero-hardcoding dynamic derivation:** `tools/round42-selfdesc-reconcile.py` dynamically computes `maxn = max(rounds)` from `^## Round (\d+)`. It derives the score vector and stagnation status rather than requiring manual transcription.
- **The Self-Locking Ratchet:** As soon as an L1 appends `## Round N`, `maxn` increments, which immediately fails `banner` and `planned` unless the author updates `Latest measured round: N` and points `Planned` to `### Planned (round N+1)`. This completely prevents the 30-to-40-round drift seen across siblings.
- **Pointer-indirection architecture:** `## Planned` in `kama` does not duplicate long round plans; it holds an authoritative pointer: `The live plan is ### Planned (round <maxN+1>) at the end of this file`, which the counter validates.
