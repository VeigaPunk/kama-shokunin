# F10C1PidOwnership — C1 pid ownership falsifier

The frozen C1 sentence says one lane-owned child serves the port; the implementation's check is narrower: `listener_inodes(port)` parses `/proc/net/tcp{,6}` for LISTEN sockets, then `pid_owns_listener(pid, port)` accepts if *any* fd under `/proc/<pid>/fd` symlinks to one of those socket inodes (`tools/kama-ollama-live.py:114-142`). This proves descriptor possession at sampling time, not that the pinned executable is the process accepting requests or that it exclusively owns the listener.

Same-user counter-arrangement: a launcher/pinned Ollama child (the inspected PID and `/proc/<pid>/exe`) creates/binds the listening socket, then forks/duplicates that fd to a separate worker process; the launcher closes its copy while the worker accepts and serves HTTP. The kernel listener inode remains identical and is reported as LISTEN, and the worker's descriptor points to `socket:[inode]`; if the inspected PID retains a duplicate, the current check passes despite the request-serving process being separate. More directly, FD inheritance lets both processes hold the same inode, so inode matching cannot distinguish who accepts. A hostile same-user process can also pass a socket FD over Unix SCM_RIGHTS to an otherwise unrelated process.

This is a demonstrated limitation of the predicate's semantics by source inspection, not a live exploit or a claim that stock Ollama v0.40.11 daemonizes/forks. The committed receipt's `listener-owned=true` only records that the sampled child had a matching descriptor; it does not establish exclusive process ownership. W1 A07 reports concurrent-run isolation and kill behavior, not this descriptor-transfer/process-identity distinction. No live probe was run; no lane source changed.
